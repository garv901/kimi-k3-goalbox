Create the most performant megakernel for one Kimi K3 text decode step using CUDA C++ and inline PTX, on two GB300 NVL72 trays in one NVLink domain: 8 NVIDIA GB300 GPUs (B300 silicon, sm_103a; check with nvidia-smi in a gpu-run request), 4 per tray. Tensor parallel 8: each rank runs exactly one persistent kernel launch per step, and that launch includes all communication between ranks, including between the two trays. The model has 93 layers: KDA (Kimi delta attention) layers, gated MLA layers at 3, 7, ..., 91 and 92, a latent MoE with 896 MXFP4 experts and top-16 routing, and block attention residuals.

You can spawn parallel subagents and choose their model, but you own the integration and the correctness. Use any of the tools in your workspace and in TASK.md as you see fit.

Define a correctness check for the megakernel against eager PyTorch that is flexible enough to let you make optimizations: for example, a tolerance relative to the error of the BF16 eager model against an FP32 run, not bit-exact agreement. Refer to the model structure on Hugging Face (moonshotai/Kimi-K3, including its modeling code) as needed, and download whatever else you need (see ENVIRONMENT.md). The real checkpoint is already on the trays: GPU jobs (gpu-run) see it read-only at /weights, in the per-rank layout that reference/megabench/tasks/kimi_checkpoint.py describes (load it with kimi.checkpoint_weights). Do not download the weights again. Run your check on the real weights. Describe the check in /work/CORRECTNESS.md, implement it as a script in /work, and run it on every change you keep.

The harness interface, the cases and the benchmark's own correctness band are in TASK.md. ./check_candidate --case ID runs the launch audit, that correctness band and the timing, and it must pass on every graded case. Each real-weight check loads about 200 GB per GPU, so do most iterations on the synthetic and development cases. ./sol_info --case ID --json gives the per-GPU HBM floor, and BASELINES.json has the vLLM latency for each graded case. To see where the time goes: ./nsys_candidate --case ID runs Nsight Systems on every rank, ./profile_candidate --case ID runs Nsight Compute, and megatimer.cuh with ./phase_timeline --case ID records clock64 and %globaltimer timestamps inside your kernel and turns them into a per-rank, per-layer timeline; the graded build never includes those timestamps. GPU jobs go through gpu-run, and a TP8 request waits for two whole trays, so batch your experiments.

Keep /work/PROGRESS.md up to date: for each attempt, the idea, the latencies before and after, kept or reverted, and why. Commit each kept change with its measured latencies.

The goal is met when the committed megakernel passes your correctness check on the real weights and ./check_candidate on every graded case on a fresh run, and five consecutive optimization attempts each improved latency by less than 2% on every graded case. Until then, keep optimizing. Record the final latency of each graded case, its ratio to the floor, its speedup over BASELINES.json and the commit hash in PROGRESS.md.
